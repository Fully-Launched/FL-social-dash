# Story over value

Core doctrine for every skill that shapes content — `concept-engine`,
`script-writer`, `positioning-doc`, `interview-questions-doc`. This isn't a
new rule bolted onto V.I.D.; it's the mechanism *behind* V.I.D.'s Identity
pillar, stated plainly enough that it directs what material gets extracted
in the first place, not just how it gets packaged afterward.

## The claim

Content doesn't perform because it's valuable. It performs because someone
watching sees themselves in it — recognizes their own week, their own
struggle, their own dream, being lived out by someone else on screen. That
recognition is what gets something screenshotted, sent to a friend, watched
twice. A tip can be correct and still not land. A story someone feels
personally addressed by lands even when it teaches nothing new.

Value still matters, but it's the second move, not the first. Value earns
its place once it's attached to a specific pain point a specific person
already recognizes in themselves — the story is what makes them lean in,
the value is what makes leaning in worth their time. Value with no story
underneath it reads as generic advice. A story with no value underneath it
still worked; it just didn't compound.

## What this means for extraction, concretely

Every skill in this repo that pulls raw material from a client should be
listening for **experiences, not just opinions**: a specific week, a
specific call, a specific trial, a specific piece of advice that landed at
the right moment. `transcript-mine`'s Stories category and
`authenticity-questions.md`'s story-drawing questions exist because of this
doctrine directly — they're not a nice-to-have alongside the "real"
extraction work, they're pointed at the highest-leverage material there is.

For `interview-questions-doc` specifically: when building questions for a
client Tait is filming, drawing out a personal story that a viewer can see
their own situation inside of is the priority the question set optimizes
for — more than efficiently covering a checklist of pain points. A question
that gets a specific, concrete story beats three questions that each get a
generic opinion.

## What this doesn't mean

This isn't permission to invent a relatable scene the client never lived.
Every story still has to trace back to something real in `voice.md`,
`customer-data.md`, or a mined source — the doctrine changes what's worth
asking for and worth pulling out, not the inviolable rule that nothing gets
manufactured. See `templates/anti-slop-checklist.md`.

## How to check a concept or question against this

Before a concept ships, or before an interview question gets asked: does
answering this produce a scene, a moment, a specific thing that happened —
or does it produce an opinion that could have come from anyone in this
niche? The first is usable. The second needs a follow-up question or a
different source before it's ready.
