# The V.I.D. Method

Shared doctrine. Every skill that produces a content concept (`concept-engine`,
`script-writer`, `filming-brief`) checks its output against this before it's
considered done. If a concept can't answer all three, it's not ready —
send it back, don't ship it.

## Visual hook — stop the scroll in 2 seconds

The first frame (or first line, on a text-first platform) has to make someone
stop mid-thumb. This is packaging, not substance — informed by
`research-sweep` (what's working in the niche right now, what formats are
outlier-performing) but never by inventing a claim the client didn't make.

Bad: a talking head starting with "Hey guys, so today I wanted to talk about—"
Good: cold open on the result, the conflict, or the question the viewer is
already asking themselves.

## Identity — "this is for me"

The pillar everyone skips, and the one that gets content screenshotted and
sent to a friend. A viewer decides in the first few seconds whether this
content is *about someone like them*. That recognition — not a tip, not a
hack — is what makes something worth saving.

Identity has to come from the client's own words: how they describe their
actual customer, in the actual language that customer uses about their own
situation (from `customer-data-doc`'s output, or mined directly from calls
in `transcript-mine`). Nobody screenshots a tip. People screenshot the post
that sounds like it was written specifically to them.

The mechanism behind this pillar is its own doctrine —
`templates/story-over-value.md`. Short version: a story someone recognizes
their own week inside of outperforms a correct tip, because recognition is
what Identity actually is. Extraction skills should be listening for
experiences and specific moments, not just opinions and pain points.

## Drive action — one clear ask

Every piece of content asks the viewer to do exactly one thing next. Not
three. One. Comment a word, click the link, book the call, follow for part
two — pick one per concept and build the whole piece toward it. A concept
with two asks has zero asks; the viewer does nothing.

## How this shows up per skill

- **concept-engine**: every concept lists its Visual hook, its Identity
  statement (who this is for and why they'll recognize themselves), and its
  Drive action, explicitly, as three labeled lines. If any line can't be
  filled from `brain.md` or mined sources, the concept doesn't go to the
  owner-approval gate — it goes back with a note on what's missing.
- **script-writer**: the hook line in the script must match the Visual hook
  from the approved concept — no drifting to a punchier line that isn't
  grounded in the client's material.
- **filming-brief**: the Identity line becomes direction to the talent (tone,
  who they should imagine they're talking to) so what gets filmed still
  lands the way the concept intended.
